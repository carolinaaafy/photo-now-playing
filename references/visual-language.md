# Photo-Derived Hand-Drawn Accent Language

Read this reference only when the user explicitly requests hand drawing, doodles, stickers, or similar decoration. Never activate it by default. Treat drawing as optional compositional notation, not a required decoration layer. Use colored-pencil or crayon marks with irregular pressure, open contours, slightly wobbly curves, and small internal details.

## Derive the Motif

Identify concrete nouns and motion in the photo: snow, leaves, wind, water, birds, chairs, flowers, a camera, a bicycle, food, reflected light, or another recognizable source detail. Choose **one semantic family**. For the default light treatment, use one small motif plus one or two related motion traces. Add a second family only when the user explicitly asks for a more decorative treatment.

Do not default to Y2K stars, hearts, bows, sparkles, or orbit symbols. Use them only when they are genuinely supported by the source or song.

## Color and Weight

- Choose one source-derived hue family from a meaningful scene anchor such as clothing, foliage, architecture, shadows, or weather. Do not promote a tiny incidental color, such as a lip tint, into the drawing palette unless it materially belongs to the whole scene.
- Adjust every mark for its own placement area. Keep the hue family consistent, but use a lighter value or lower opacity on dark regions and a darker value on pale regions. Do not force one identical swatch across all marks.
- Prefer the lightest locally visible value. The drawing should be noticed after the photograph and player, not before them. Reject a motif whose darkness makes it read as a logo or app icon.
- For the light treatment, make the main motif about **4–7% of canvas width** and secondary traces about **1.5–3%**. Use medium-fine grainy strokes; avoid thick marker lines and large filled shapes.
- Use white only where the underlying photo is dark enough to retain contrast.
- Test local contrast, not palette in isolation. If the motif disappears when the image is viewed at 25% size, increase saturation, line weight, or scale before adding more motifs.

## Placement

- Place the main motif in true breathing room with clear local contrast. Prefer open background space over any contact with the player. Keep a visible gap from every player edge unless the user explicitly requests overlap and the drawing is composited on a separate layer without altering the locked player mask. Use related motion traces to create one directional path through the composition.
- Cluster related marks loosely rather than distributing isolated icons evenly around the border.
- Allow one short echo mark to tuck behind the foreground subject or player edge when it strengthens depth, but keep the main motif readable.
- Keep all marks off faces, full-body contours, readable player text, album art, and important architectural details.

## Avoid

- More than 3 marks in the light treatment or more than 2 motif families in any treatment.
- One motif much darker or more saturated than its related traces without a local-contrast reason.
- Full outlines around a person or pet.
- A single tiny low-contrast mark that reads as compression noise.
- Uniform icons, heavy black strokes, thick neon marker treatment, dense sticker clusters, or generic symbols with no relation to the photo.
